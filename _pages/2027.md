  <p>Agentic artificial intelligence is driving a profound transformation in science. By enabling systems that can reason, plan, and execute complex research tasks with increasing autonomy, agentic AI is redefining the scientific process itself. Applied mathematics has proven particularly well suited to this emerging paradigm, where structured reasoning and programming converge.</p>
  <p>The 2027 Geilo Winter School explores the future of agentic computational science. Through hands-on sessions, case studies, and discussions of emerging trends, we will examine how AI agents are changing the way we work. The school will provide both practical tools and a strategic perspective on the opportunities ahead, in a field that evolves at unprecedented speed.</p>

## Topics

### Trustworthy research with agentic AI (Geir Kjetil Sandve)

*Lecture 1: A second brain for your research, built with agentic AI*

Much of what makes a researcher effective never ends up in a paper: the articles
you have read and what you thought of them, your own notes and drafts, the
topics you know well enough to build on, your opinions about where your field is
heading, and figures, explanations and plans. Agentic AI makes it practical to
collect all of this into a personal knowledge base that grows over time. It also
lets the AI draw on that knowledge base when it helps you write, plan or
analyse. A short introduction (20–30 min) shows some ideas for how such a
"second brain" can be organised and maintained, and where the AI should and
should not take the lead. Participants then set up a first version for their own
research and discuss design choices with each other and in plenary exchanges.

*Lecture 2: Outer harnesses for transparent and reproducible research with agentic AI*

An AI agent will happily run thirty analyses and report the best one. The
question is how to make it do research you can actually trust. This lecture is
about the "outer harness": the skills, instruction documents, conventions and
checks that can help steer an agent towards analyses that are transparent,
reproducible and stable to reasonable alternative choices. A short introduction
(20–30 min) is followed by participants building a small harness for an analysis
of their own, then discussion in plenary.

*Lecture 3: Setting the direction for machine-learning method development with agentic AI*

Agentic AI can generate and test machine-learning methods far faster than a
person can, which makes it easy to end up with many variants and no clear idea
of which results to believe. This lecture is about setting the direction before
the agent starts. Datasets, evaluation set-ups and ways of working are
established first, so that you end up with a method whose results you trust and
a clear overview of the methods developed, how they performed and how the
development went. A short introduction (20–30 min) draws on concrete examples
from immunology and time-series forecasting. Participants then set up such a
framework for a method-development problem of their own and discuss it with each
other.

    <h3 style="margin-top: 0;">Geir Kjetil Sandve</h3>
    <p>Geir Kjetil Sandve is Professor of Machine Learning at the Department of Informatics, University of Oslo, and co-director of TRUST, the Norwegian Centre for Trustworthy AI. His research spans methodology development and applied work, with a particular focus on deep learning, spatio-temporal modeling, simulation, benchmarking and reproducibility, with a growing focus on veridical data science and on how agentic AI changes the practice of research. On the application side he works on immune-receptor repertoire analysis, through the open-source immuneML platform, and on climate-sensitive disease forecasting in low- and middle-income countries, through the Chap modelling platform.</p>
